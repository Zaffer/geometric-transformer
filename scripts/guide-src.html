1The screen

The screen has one 3D scene and four flat panels. The 3D scene fills the window. The panels float above it.

top bar
The step count, the loss, the accuracy, and the renderer. The reset camera button fits the scene to the window. The open tutorial button opens this guide. The menu button (☰) gives the dark or light theme.
left panel
The controls for the model, the training, the sample, and the view.
right panel
The element that you clicked in the scene. Here you edit its parameters.
bottom panel
Two charts: the training loss and the sort accuracy.
circuit panel
The lower part of the scene. It shows every neuron and every synapse for one position.
sequence panel
The upper part of the scene, further back. It shows all positions, the attention, and the predictions.

The camera

  • Drag with the left mouse button to rotate the scene.
  • Drag with the right mouse button to move the scene.
  • Turn the mouse wheel to zoom.
  • Click reset camera to see the full scene again.
Note

The camera does not move by itself. A change of the model or of the weights keeps your view.

2The task

The model learns to sort letters. The letters are A, B, and C. One example has 6 random input letters, and then the same letters in sorted order. The model reads the sequence one letter at a time. At each position, it predicts the next letter.

The sequence in the scene has 11 positions: the 6 input letters and the first 5 sorted letters. At the last position, the model predicts the 6th sorted letter. Example:

BABBAA AAABB → B

The first 6 letters are the input. The next 5 letters are the sorted letters, so far. The correct next letter is B. Positions 5 to 10 have a correct answer. Positions 0 to 4 have no correct answer, because the input letters are random.

The default model is small: 2 blocks, 2 attention heads, a width of 16, and 6816 parameters. The scene shows all of them.

3The circuit panel: the rules

The circuit panel shows the complete computation for one position. Read it from left to right. Each column of spheres is one step of the computation. The label above a column names the step.

sphere
One neuron. A neuron holds one number. Orange is positive. Blue is negative. Dark is near zero. The number is the value at the selected position.
line
One synapse. A synapse is one weight of the model. Green is positive. Red is negative. A thick, bright line is a large weight. A thin, dark line is near zero.
diamond
One bias node. Each line from a diamond is one bias of the model.
thin gray line
A copy of a value to the next column. It has no parameter.
cube
One attention head. See section 5.
Note

The weights are the same for all 11 positions. Only the sphere colors change with the position. The circuit position slider selects the position. The default is position 10, the last one.

4The input part

token
3 spheres: A, B, and C. One sphere is lit. It is the letter at the selected position.
position
11 spheres. One sphere is lit. It is the position number, from 0 at the top to 10 at the bottom.
embed
16 spheres. This is the residual stream: the vector that flows through the whole model. Each value is the sum of one token embedding value and one position embedding value.

The lines from token to embed are the token embedding matrix: 3 × 16 = 48 weights. The lines from position to embed are the position embedding matrix: 11 × 16 = 176 weights. Only the lit sphere sends its values forward. The other lines show the weights, but they carry zero.

5The attention part

ln1
Layer normalization. 16 spheres. Each sphere has two parameters: a gain and a bias. Click a sphere to see them.
q, k, v
Three columns of 16 spheres in one tall stack: the queries, the keys, and the values. The lines from ln1 are one matrix: 16 × 48 = 768 weights.
cubes
One cube for each attention head. Head 0 is cyan. Head 1 is violet. Each head owns 8 of the 16 q, k, and v spheres. The q and k spheres of a head connect to its cube. The cube stands for the attention weights that q and k produce together.
att mix
16 spheres. The values (v) of this position and of all earlier positions, mixed with the attention weights. Thin gray lines join v and the cubes to this column.
att out
16 spheres, after the output matrix (16 × 16 = 256 weights) and its bias.
+
The residual sum: the input of the block plus att out. Thin gray lines bring both.
Note

The circuit panel shows one position. The mixing across positions is not visible here. The sequence panel shows it as arcs (section 9).

6The MLP part

ln2
The second layer normalization. 16 spheres with a gain and a bias each.
mlp gelu
64 spheres. Each sphere is one hidden neuron after the GELU activation function. The lines from ln2 are 16 × 64 = 1024 weights. This column is the part of the model that bends the space. It is the subject of the later geometry views.
mlp out
16 spheres, after the projection matrix (64 × 16 = 1024 weights) and its bias.
+
The residual sum. This vector is the output of the block. It enters the next block, or the final normalization.

One block has 3280 parameters. Two blocks have 6560. With the embeddings and the final normalization, the model has 6816.

7The output part

ln f
The final layer normalization.
logits
3 spheres: A, B, and C. The score for each letter. The brightest orange sphere is the prediction. The softmax of these three values gives the probabilities in the sequence panel.

The lines from ln f to logits are the unembedding matrix: 16 × 3 = 48 weights. When the tie embed weights switch is on, these are the same numbers as the token embedding weights of section 4. The scene draws them in both places. If you edit one, the other changes too.

8The bias nodes

A gray diamond stands beside and below the column that it feeds. Each line from the diamond to a sphere is one bias value. The colors and the thickness follow the same rule as the weights.

Each block has four diamonds: for q, k, and v (48 biases), for att out (16), for mlp gelu (64), and for mlp out (16). The layer normalizations keep their biases inside the spheres. Click a sphere of ln1, ln2, or ln f to see them.

9The sequence panel

The sequence panel shows all 11 positions at the same time. Position 0 is at the left. Read the panel from the bottom to the top.

tokens
One cube for each letter of the sequence. Teal is A. Violet is B. Yellow is C. The letters are below the cubes.
embed
One column of 16 cells for each position. This is the residual stream after the embedding. The cell colors follow the sphere rule: orange positive, blue negative.
att 0
The attention arcs of block 0. See Figure 9.
block 0
The residual stream after block 0, for each position.
att 1, block 1
The same for block 1.
predict
The letter with the highest probability at each position. A gray letter has no correct answer. A green letter is correct. A red letter is wrong.
bars
At the right of the predict row: the three probabilities A, B, and C at the last position. The tallest bar is the final prediction.

One arc joins an earlier position to a later position. It means: the later position reads from the earlier one. A bright arc is a strong attention weight. A dark arc is a weak one. Cyan arcs belong to head 0. Violet arcs belong to head 1. The arcs reach only to the left, because a position cannot see the future.

Note

The arcs show the attention for the current example sequence. Click new sequence to see a different example.

The charts

The left chart shows the training loss. The right chart shows the sort accuracy. Each chart shows the last value and the number of points. Drag the bar above the charts to change their height.

11How to edit one parameter

  1. Click pause if the training runs. If not, the optimizer overwrites your edit at the next step.
  2. Click a line or a sphere in the circuit panel. A yellow marker shows the selected element.
  3. Read the name in the right panel. Example: wte[2, 2] is row 2, column 2 of the token embedding matrix. Row 2 is the letter C. Column 2 is the third value of the embedding.
  4. Move the slider. The scene updates at once: the line changes its color and its thickness, and all sphere colors after it change.
  5. Click new sequence to see the effect on a different example.

Spheres with parameters: the ln spheres (a gain and a bias), and the spheres of q, k, v, att out, mlp gelu, and mlp out (one bias each). The spheres of token, position, embed, att mix, +, and logits have no parameters. They show values only.

Tip

Thin lines are hard to click. Increase edge thickness in the view folder, or zoom in with the mouse wheel.
